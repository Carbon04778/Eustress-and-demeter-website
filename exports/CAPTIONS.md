## 1. The Scalability Pyramid
Files: `portrait-1-pyramid.png` / `instagram-square-1-pyramid.png`

Every restaurant brand I have worked with wanted the same thing: more locations.

Almost none of them wanted to talk about what sits underneath them.

Foundation. Processes. Leadership. Consistency. Only then, scalable growth.

Each layer holds the one above it. Skip a layer and the structure carries weight it was never built for. That is not a metaphor, it is what a failed second location actually is.

Where is your brand thinnest?

## 2. Five readiness questions
Files: `portrait-2-questions.png` / `instagram-square-2-questions.png`

Before you sign another lease, answer these five honestly.

Can the business run without you.
Are training systems documented.
Can managers decide without you.
Is the guest experience consistent.
Can performance be measured the same way everywhere.

Most founders can answer two of five. Two of five is not a green light, it is a work order.

If you got stuck on one of them, that is the one to fix first.

---

## 3. Five signs you are not ready to scale
Files: `portrait-3-five-signs.png` / `instagram-square-3-five-signs.png`

The owner still approves everything.
Training is not written down.
Guest experience varies by shift.
Every manager runs the store differently.
Reporting is not standardized.

If more than two are true, the next location will not fix them. It will copy them.

Scaling exposes operational weaknesses. It does not solve them.

---

## 4. Franchising is a replication strategy
Files: `portrait-4-replication.png` / `instagram-square-4-replication.png`

Franchising is not a growth strategy. It is a replication strategy.

Whatever your operation is on the day you sell the first territory is what gets copied into every market after it. The good and the unfinished.

Restaurant, then systems, then consistency, then leadership. Franchise ready comes last, not first.

Franchisees inherit your systems, not your intentions.

## 5. From the floor
Files: `portrait-5-editorial.png` / `instagram-square-5-editorial.png`

What I noticed this week visiting restaurants.

The best operators were not the busiest ones. They were the ones whose staff did not need to look for them.

Nobody was hovering by the pass waiting for a decision. Nobody was tracking down the owner to approve a comp. The systems were doing the work that the owner would otherwise be doing personally, and you could feel it in how calm the room was.

That is what a documented operation looks like from the dining room.
